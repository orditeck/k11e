How to protect your online privacy

Start with passwords, recovery and backups; then choose browsing, messaging and network tools for the risks they actually address.

Start with the basics

This guide started in 2023. Product names below are examples, not a current security audit or a shopping list you need to complete. Check the linked documentation before choosing tools. Start with the accounts and devices you rely on most; adding a VPN while leaving your email easy to take over is the wrong order.

First, protect access and recovery:

  1. Use a different strong password for each account. A password manager can help you keep track of them.
  2. Enable two-factor authentication where available, especially for your email and password manager. Save recovery codes securely and check how to regain access if you lose a device or key. More on hardware keys below.
  3. Keep devices, browsers and apps updated.
  4. Keep independent backups of important data and check that you can restore them. Sync can copy an accidental deletion too. Protect backups against unwanted access, and keep any encryption recovery key safe and separate.

Then reduce everyday exposure:

  1. Be cautious with unexpected links, attachments and requests to sign in. Go to a service directly when in doubt.
  2. Review what you share, service privacy settings and app permissions. Download software from sources you trust.
  3. On shared computers, avoid saving credentials and sign out when finished. A public computer may still be compromised, even after you sign out.

The next sections address different problems. None replaces these basics, and none makes everything you do anonymous.

Reduce ads, tracking and phishing exposure

An ad or content blocker can reduce exposure to some ads and third-party trackers. Depending on the tool and its filter lists, it may also block some known malicious domains. It won't catch every tracker, scam or phishing page, and some sites may break until you adjust the settings.

AdGuard is one option, with different products and features depending on the device. Check what a particular app or DNS service actually filters. Parental filters and anti-tracking settings aren't a substitute for judgment or supervision.

Decide whether you need a VPN

What is a VPN?

A virtual private network creates an encrypted connection between your device and a VPN server. Your local network can see that you're using the VPN, but generally not the traffic inside that tunnel. The VPN provider handles your traffic after it leaves the tunnel, so you're shifting some trust from your network provider to the VPN provider. HTTPS still matters between you and a website. See the EFF's guide to choosing a VPN.

Why use a VPN

A VPN may be useful for reaching a private network or changing the IP address a site sees. Depending on the network and service, it may also help access blocked content. It doesn't make you anonymous: websites can still recognize signed-in accounts and use other identifiers, and the VPN provider may have access to connection information. A VPN can't provide access without a working route to its server, and it doesn't guarantee protection from malware or phishing.

Public Wi-Fi isn't automatically unsafe. HTTPS protects the connection to a properly authenticated website, including on a public network. Keep devices updated rather than treating a VPN as a cure-all. See the FTC's public Wi-Fi guidance.

VPN + AdGuard?

A VPN and a content blocker do different jobs: one routes traffic through a server, while the other filters some requests or page content. Whether they work together depends on the apps and device; using both doesn't prevent all tracking or attacks. Check the relevant setup instructions instead of assuming both protections are active.

Choose end-to-end encrypted messaging

End-to-end encryption is designed so that message content is readable by the communicating devices, not the messaging service. It does not hide all metadata or protect messages on a compromised or unlocked device.

Signal uses end-to-end encryption for its messages and calls by default. Telegram's FAQ distinguishes ordinary cloud chats from end-to-end encrypted Secret Chats. Secret Chats are one-to-one and aren't the default. Choose deliberately, and consider who can access the devices and backups at either end.

Use a privacy-focused browser

Firefox and Brave offer tracker-protection features. Review their settings and keep whichever browser you choose updated. Private browsing mainly limits what is stored in the local browsing history; it does not make you anonymous to websites or your network.

Brave also offers private windows with Tor connectivity, but that is not a guarantee of anonymity. If anonymity is important, consult the Tor Project's guidance. Mozilla also offers a browser comparison. Check current support and behavior rather than assuming an old feature description still applies.

Use two-factor authentication

What is two-factor authentication?

Two-factor authentication adds another factor to a login, such as a security key or an authenticator code in addition to a password. It makes a stolen password less useful, although some methods can still be phished or bypassed through account recovery. See CISA's MFA guidance.

YubiKey

A YubiKey is one example of a hardware security key. Where an account supports FIDO security keys, they can offer phishing-resistant authentication. USB and NFC options depend on the key, device and service; follow the account's enrollment instructions.

Plan for loss before relying on a key: register a second key where supported, and store recovery codes securely. A backup key is a way to recover access, not an additional factor on every login. Check each account's recovery methods too, since those can affect the overall protection.

Consider moving away from the big tech giants

Changing providers can reduce reliance on a single company, but a smaller provider is not automatically private or secure. Compare its data practices, security, export options, cost and your ability to recover the account. You do not have to move everything at once.
