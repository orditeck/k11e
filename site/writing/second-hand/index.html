Second-hand, taxed again: my frustration with online reselling

An unnecessary MacBook purchase, resale fees and a buyer's tax screenshot left me wondering why reusing things has to feel so expensive.

Written by
Keven Lefebvre
Originally published
December 20, 2023
Language
English

I turned to eBay to sell a 14-inch MacBook Pro M1 Max I'd bought from a friend. Where I live, finding a buyer for a high-end laptop can take patience, and I wasn't sure I'd get the price I wanted locally.

When my friend mentioned the MacBook was for sale, I jumped at it. I'd had a string of frustrating experiences with Intel laptops and wanted to give Apple Silicon another shot, even though work had already provided me with one. I'm a serial tech buyer, always chasing the next best thing and not always sure what I actually need. 🤦😬

Around here, a snowblower or a snowmobile seems to move faster than a top-tier laptop. 🤣 So I listed the MacBook on eBay for $2,500. I'd paid $2,300, taxes included, but an eBay fees calculator suggested I'd end up with around $2,100 or even less. Even at my asking price, I was looking at an estimated loss after fees, not a profit.

That's partly on me. I didn't really need the laptop, and I knew I wasn't the biggest fan of macOS, but the hardware won me over. I'm rarely quite satisfied with an electronic device I own. Story of my life.

Then a potential buyer asked for more photos, which I sent. They asked for my best price, and I explained how much the eBay fees would take. That's when they told me eBay was also charging tax and sent a screenshot. I was speechless—and angry.

I'd already paid tax when I bought the laptop. Seeing another charge added for the buyer felt excessive, and it made me wonder how we'd find a price that worked for both of us. The fees squeezed my side of the transaction; the tax increased what the buyer would pay. Those are different charges, but both mattered to the price we were discussing.

I dug into it. eBay says that, effective July 1, 2022, it collects Canadian sales taxes on sales of tangible goods within Canada from Canada-based buyers on behalf of Canada-based sellers, irrespective of the seller's registration status. The tax shown depends on the transaction and destination; my buyer's screenshot doesn't establish the tax treatment of every sale.

My first reaction didn't capture the whole picture. The CRA's digital-economy guidance distinguishes, for qualifying goods, collection by a registered vendor from collection by a distribution platform operator, depending on the circumstances. A service that only lists or advertises goods is not the same as an operator involved in the transaction. Revenu Québec also says used property is generally taxable, while a sale by someone not engaged in commercial activities, such as an individual selling personal items at a garage sale, is not taxable; there are exceptions. That example doesn't settle my eBay listing.

A later distinction is worth keeping separate: the CRA's platform reporting rules require certain platforms to report information about reportable sellers. Reporting is not the same as collecting sales tax, and neither tells an individual seller by itself what income tax, if any, they owe. These sources explain distinctions, not a determination of my own obligations.

I'm still frustrated, even if I can't say the charge was unlawful. I understand the need to tax commerce, including sales by foreign businesses. But I'd also like it to be easier to reuse things instead of buying new ones. Another charge at checkout made me worry that buying second-hand would be less appealing; my one listing doesn't tell me what buyers generally choose to do. And this account doesn't establish a completed sale or its final price.

I keep coming back to the same question: if we want people to reuse things, shouldn't we make that easier? I don't have an answer for every tax situation. I just know how strange it felt to see another tax on a used laptop I'd already paid tax on.

What's your take on this? Have you had similar experiences selling second-hand items online?
