A practical experiment

AI in your day-to-day work

An interesting demo is not yet a working process. If you have a repetitive task, documents to work through or an automation idea, we can look at how things happen now and what is worth trying. AI might help; a simpler tool might be the better choice.

  • Find a starting point. Examine the need, available tools, data and what would make a result useful.
  • Try a real, bounded case. Build a prototype, small automation or experiment that can be compared with the way you work today.
  • Decide what to keep. Check errors and difficult cases, document what works, then decide whether to continue, adjust or stop.

That can be a working session, a guided experiment or a small development engagement. You do not need to buy a large project to examine an idea.

A hands-on workshop

Practice AI with your development team

Already using coding assistants, or want to try them without confusing speed with quality? We can prepare a practical workshop around a representative development task. The point is not to memorize magic prompts, but to know what to ask, what to check and when to take over.

We can work in your repository if access and tool-use permissions allow it, or prepare an example without confidential code or data. A session can follow a change from beginning to end:

  1. Read the context and frame a task precisely enough to evaluate its result.
  2. Produce a small change with the assistant, then inspect the code and proposed choices.
  3. Test the important cases, look for mistakes and review what remains uncertain.
  4. Document the change and checks the team can repeat without me.

The aim is to leave with an annotated example, reusable checks and working practices that fit your team. We agree on the artifacts, starting level, duration and format together, before the session.

I enjoy teaching while building with people. My career story includes supporting developers as we learned a new stack together. Trying things, explaining the choices and leaving people better equipped for what comes next: that is the way I want to work with your team.

Working together

Who is this for?

Entrepreneurs, businesses, teams and individuals with a project who are ready to invest time and resources in it. You don't need all the answers—or the name of the right tool—before writing to me. Curiosity, context and a willingness to work together are a good start.

Keep judgment and data in the right places

AI can be wrong, inconsistent or unsuitable for a task. A person remains responsible for reviewing the result, especially when it informs an important decision. We agree on checks before treating a convincing output as a good answer.

Some tools send code or information to outside providers. We discuss permissions, data sensitivity and tool choices before using them. Please do not send confidential information in your first email.
