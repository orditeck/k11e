A game, a company, and the people along the way

From learning PHP to building for clients and working with larger teams.

Updated
November 17, 2026

Before I was building software for other people, I wanted to make a game. I was learning PHP and started Orkïka, a text-based medieval game in the browser. There was a lot I didn't know yet. Having something I wanted to make gave me a reason to keep asking questions.

I found a community of people making games with PHP. Over time, I helped run the forum with other people as it became JeuPHP and later JeuWeb. There were discussions about code and game ideas, but also the less visible work of keeping a place for people to meet: servers, backups and moderation.

The forum, Orkïka and the Ultima Online server I hosted were different projects. They all gave me something to try, break, fix or talk through with someone else. The two computers under my old monitor were part of that period, too.

Building for other people

I'd been working with the web before I officially registered my business in 2008, at 17. Through OrbiteWeb, the work grew from websites to online stores and custom applications for businesses. Some clients came back with new projects; the conversation rarely ended with the first site. Their needs evolved, and so did the solutions.

A website might be the part everyone sees, but it isn't always the whole problem. In a 2016 interview about OrbiteWeb, I described businesses working with paper forms and client files, and applications that could make information available across departments. The interesting question was not just what a page should look like; it was how people needed the information to move.

Sometimes an existing tool was a good starting point. Sometimes the work needed something more custom. I liked being able to use what's already useful and make the missing pieces fit the people doing the work.

I also like writing documentation. And I love good code partly because I love reading good code. Someone—including future me—has to come back and understand what we made.

During the OrbiteWeb years, I also worked with the development team at La Web Shop. My own client work and work alongside another team overlapped; they weren't separate, neatly numbered chapters.

Joining other teams

I'd collaborated with Arsenal Web before OrbiteWeb joined the agency in 2018. I joined as Director of Technology. Working on technical foundations and development workflows meant thinking about more than one project at a time, and helping other developers with their work too.

That work also included introducing open-source tools. Around the same period, on a separate contract, I worked alongside a municipal technology team as they learned a new stack while we built services together.

Later, at Turbulent, I worked with a core development team across different projects. Architecture and sharing knowledge were part of the job, but so was jumping in when a difficult problem needed attention. I used to call that part firefighting.

Then I joined Shopify. It was my first job working in English, and I wanted to learn from people who would challenge me. Working with colleagues from different specialties gave me more ways to look at a problem—and more chances to explain what I was thinking.

I love learning from people, and I enjoy teaching, too. I've had the chance to help other developers learn a new stack while we built things together. There is something satisfying about figuring out a real problem and leaving the people working on it better equipped for the next one.

Still making things

There isn't one straight line from a browser game to a job title. What connects these chapters for me is the work in between: understanding what someone is trying to do, finding a place to start, and figuring things out with other people.

I still make things for myself, too. Inkjack and Daraoké are two recent examples. I love singing and playing with the tools used to record and mix music. I'm learning about songwriting, and I want the words I sing to mean something to me. I'm not ready to share my music yet, but it belongs in the picture—along with the outdoors and whatever else catches my attention.
